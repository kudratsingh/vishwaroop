## 0. Scope and limits

1. **M5-early is offline preparation, not the M5 gate (owner decision, 2026-11-08).** M5-early covers design, tooling, and the real-state scorer's accuracy and latency on real logged data. None of it counts toward an M5 exit test. The formal M5 gate is unchanged in `config/phases.json`: M5 keeps M2b as its prerequisite, and its exit tests wait for M2b. They are ET-M5-1 ("Real-arm success versus N is measured with paired trials against N=1 and the GRPO-tuned policy"; threshold to be frozen when the phase becomes next) and ET-M5-2 ("Compute per episode and the N at which gains stop are reported"). No `phases.json` edit or ADR is requested for phase order.
2. **What offline evidence can and cannot show (`AGENTS.md §7.3, §13.6`).** Real logs validate executed chunks only. Offline work can establish the scorer's accuracy, calibration, abstention, hard-violation detection and latency on chunks the arm actually executed. It cannot show that the scorer ranks *alternative, unexecuted* candidates correctly on the real arm. That needs intervention evidence: simulator branches (sim only, already done in M1) or the paired physical trial in §4. It also cannot show how the arm physically behaves in a safe stop (§3.2, §3.4). Offline success is a necessary gate for proposing a trial, not a claim about best-of-N.

## 1. The sharpened question and what prior work already answered

### 1.1 What the field has already shown (verified 2026-11-08 from the papers' own pages)

| Work | What it showed | What it leaves open for us |
|---|---|---|
| **DREAM-Chunk** (Chen et al., arXiv 2606.18589, submitted 2026-06-17, https://arxiv.org/html/2606.18589) | Test-time selection among N sampled chunks with a latent world model, no policy fine-tuning, **on an SO-101** with a fine-tuned SmolVLA: N = 10, 20 rollouts per condition, 50 to 100 teleop demos per task; USB unplug/insert 75% → 95%, pick moving object 60% → 80%, pick-and-insert toy 35% → 45%. SO-101/SmolVLA latency was measured on an RTX 5090 workstation. On a Franka with π0.5, remote inference scored below local (40/30/35% at N = 10/15/20 vs 65% local at N = 5), which the authors attribute to added delay. The Franka/π0.5 runs were distributed: robot control on a local PC (Intel i7-11700KF, RTX 3080), inference on a remote workstation (AMD Threadripper PRO 5995WX, **RTX A6000**) over WebSocket (§5.2). π0.5 inference took 697.93 ms at N = 10, 1001.27 ms at N = 15 and 1303.82 ms at N = 20 (Table 2). These hardware and latency details were verified for the owner's 2026-11-08 ADR-0009 decision. Its selection rule matches predicted against observed state (reactive chunk switching), not an outcome score. | "Does test-time chunk selection help an SO-101 policy" is answered yes in their setting. Not reported: calibrated abstention, false optimism, a hard-safety path, a comparison with fine-tuning at matched real budget, ACT, or a state-only scorer. |
| **VeriVLA** (Sun et al., arXiv 2610.21985, submitted 2026-10-15, https://arxiv.org/html/2610.21985) | Flow-matching VLAs (π0.5 on the real robot) sampled with a distinct noise seed per candidate; a two-sample trigger decides when to verify; a JEPA latent world model ranks K = 10 candidates. Real AgileX PiperX dual arm, three tasks: completion 50.8% → 65.5%. Added latency 326 ms (80 ms extra sampling + 246 ms verifier) on one RTX 4090; 96 ms untriggered, 422 ms triggered. | A gate that falls back to the first sample when no trigger fires, but no calibrated abstention or false-optimism rate; lab-grade dual arm, not a low-cost arm; no fine-tuning comparison. |
| **Best-of-N headroom study** (Gao et al., arXiv 2610.37196, submitted 2026-10-27, https://arxiv.org/html/2610.37196) | Simulation only (RoboCasa, LIBERO-Plus; four world action models; no real robot). Native policy samples: held-out decision-level headroom ≈ 0 (+0.003 [−0.024, 0.035] on TurnOnStove). Structured perturbations (end-effector offsets along robot axes, about 2, 5 and 10 cm; orientation and gripper unchanged): +0.158 [0.081, 0.247] at κ = 0.25. A gated selector that deviates only when failure is predicted: closed loop +4.7 [1.9, 7.6] points on training tasks, +7.2 [4.7, 9.8] on unseen tasks (76.9% → 81.6%; 62.6% → 69.8%). Value head ρ = 0.01 vs future-frame probe ρ = 0.19 (0.23 pooled). Held-out oracle stays flat as candidates grow. | Whether the native-vs-structured finding holds on real, backlash-limited hardware; time-shift and gripper-timing perturbations were not tested. |
| **ACT** (Zhao et al., arXiv 2304.13705, submitted 2023-04-23, https://arxiv.org/html/2304.13705) | CVAE-trained chunk policy; "At test time, we set z to be the mean of the prior distribution i.e. zero to deterministically decode." Removing the CVAE objective dropped human-data success from 35.3% to 2%. Temporal ensembling queries the policy every step and averages overlapping chunks with exponential weights, "only extra inference-time computation". | Nobody we found reports ACT best-of-N, or whether ACT's latent carries usable diversity at test time. |

Corrections to the review's leads: DREAM-Chunk's SO-101 policy is **SmolVLA**, not ACT, and the 15M figure is the paper's general statement about JEPA world models, not the size of the model used on hardware (unverified for the hardware runs). The headroom study's "+11 to +16" is a decision-level oracle bound, and its closed-loop gains (+4.7, +7.2) come from a gated selector in simulation.

### 1.2 Sharpened questions (proposed; replaces the PROGRAM §8.2 arm row by ADR after owner review)

- **Q1 (primary). Gain versus fine-tuning at a matched real-trial budget.** On one SO-101 pick-and-place task, spend a budget of B real episodes (proposed unit: executed real episodes plus their operator minutes) either (a) on training and calibrating a real-state scorer used for best-of-N with abstention, or (b) on fine-tuning the same policy with B more real episodes (SFT on demos or interventions; RL fine-tuning only if the arm project produces one). Which gives the larger paired real-success gain over the N = 1 policy?
- **Q2. Proposal distribution and N.** On real hardware, do native samples (ACT latent; π0.5 noise seeds) or structured perturbations create selectable headroom, and where between N ∈ {1, 4, 8} do gains stop?
- **Q3. Abstention, safe stops and false optimism.** At the frozen thresholds, how often does the selector abstain in the ordinary way (execute candidate 0), how often does it call a safe stop for a hard violation, how often is it falsely optimistic, and does abstention keep the system no worse than N = 1 on out-of-range states (a no-harm test)?
- **Q4. Latency.** Does the per-decision overhead at each N fit the control budget on the bench computer, and does the pause it adds change success on its own?

What stays ours: a sub-$500 arm with measured backlash, calibrated abstention and measured false optimism as release criteria (`docs/EVALUATION_PROTOCOL.md §6`), a hard-violation path that stops rather than falls back, a state-based scorer validated on physics branches (M1), and the matched-budget comparison. Unverified lead from the review: MG-Select (arXiv 2510.05681) reports gains flat beyond N = 4; not checked here.

## 2. How ACT gets distinct candidate chunks

**Fact base (verified).** LeRobot's ACT sets the latent to zeros whenever it is not training ("When not using the VAE encoder, we set the latent to be all zeros", `modeling_act.py`, https://github.com/huggingface/lerobot/blob/main/src/lerobot/policies/act/modeling_act.py, read 2026-11-08). Defaults in `configuration_act.py` (same date): `chunk_size = 100`, `n_action_steps = 100`, `latent_dim = 32`, `kl_weight = 10.0`, `temporal_ensemble_coeff = None`; temporal ensembling requires `n_action_steps = 1`. So standard ACT is deterministic for a given observation, and two calls give identical chunks.

**Fixed rules for every option.**
- **N = 1 is the policy's own standard output:** z = 0, the exported config's `n_action_steps` and temporal-ensembling setting, no selection. It is the baseline arm and is never itself perturbed. *Proposed default:* the same hard-violation monitor (§3.2) runs in every arm, N = 1 included, so arms differ only in selection, not in their safety layer (§6, O10).
- **Candidate 0 of every set is that same z = 0 chunk.** Ordinary abstention means executing candidate 0, so an abstaining selector reduces to N = 1.
- **Candidate 0 is a fallback for low confidence, not a safe action.** If candidate 0 is predicted or detected to breach a hard constraint, it is not executed. The decision routes to a safe stop or a handoff (§3.2). This covers the case where every candidate, candidate 0 included, is predicted to violate.
- **A candidate predicted to breach a hard constraint is never selected.** A perturbed candidate whose targets leave the joint limits or the workspace is discarded, not clipped, so a selected chunk is never silently altered (*proposed default*).
- **Nested sets.** The N = 4 set is the first four of the N = 8 set, generated from a per-decision seed derived from (episode id, decision index), so arms are paired at the candidate level and reproducible.
- **Selection happens at chunk boundaries,** every `n_action_steps` steps. Temporal ensembling is not combined with best-of-N in M5-early (it would require scoring at every control step). If the exported policy was evaluated with temporal ensembling, N = 1 keeps it and the best-of-N arms state the difference; this is an owner decision (§6, O4).
- **Distinctness metric.** For candidates a⁽ⁱ⁾ over the scored prefix of H steps (6 joint targets each; gripper scaled to radians-equivalent by its range), d(i, j) = RMS over steps and arm joints of a⁽ⁱ⁾ − a⁽ʲ⁾, with the gripper reported separately. A set's distinctness is min over i ≠ j of d(i, j). *Proposed default:* a set counts as distinct if its minimum pairwise d ≥ 0.015 rad, about the STS3215 backlash the review cites (0.87°, an unverified lead) and to be replaced by the backlash we measure on our arm. Candidates closer than the arm's own backlash are not physically different. Report the full distribution, not only the pass rate.

| Option | Retraining? | Changes the policy's distribution? | How distinctness arises | How N ∈ {1, 4, 8} is formed | Cost and risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Sample the CVAE latent** z ~ N(0, τ²I), τ ∈ {0.5, 1.0} | No. An inference shim replaces the zeros with samples (our loader, built on the public `lerobot` package, never the arm project's code) | Within the learned prior at τ = 1 in principle; the deployed policy has only ever been validated at z = 0, and τ > 1 is off-distribution | Only as much as the decoder uses z. With `kl_weight = 10`, the decoder may largely ignore z (posterior collapse); this is a hypothesis to measure, not a known fact | z = 0 plus 3 or 7 samples, one batched forward pass | Cheapest (one batch of N). Risk: near-identical chunks. Measured offline on logged observations (§5, WO-C) before any trial |
| **B. Structured perturbations** of the z = 0 chunk: time shift (±k steps), end-effector target offsets (±1 and ±2 cm along table axes through SO-101 FK/IK), gripper timing (close/open ±k steps) | No | Yes, deliberately off-policy; a perturbed candidate that leaves the joint limits or the workspace is discarded | By construction; size is a parameter | z = 0 plus a fixed, pre-registered menu: N = 4 = {0, +x, −y, gripper −k}; N = 8 adds {−x, +y, shift +k, gripper +k} (illustrative; frozen later) | Cheap. The headroom study's sim evidence favours it. Risk: the scorer must be calibrated on perturbed chunks (WO-016's lesson: calibration without them abstained on 42 to 56% of perturbed chunks) |
| **C. Checkpoint or seed ensemble** | Yes: N independent training runs in the arm project | Each candidate is a different policy | Training variance | N runs, one candidate each | N× memory and forward cost; N = 8 needs 8 runs; confounds selection with ensembling and complicates the policy-family holdout. Not recommended |
| **D. Temporal-ensemble phase offsets** (chunks predicted from the last k observations) | No | Stale observations | Small; candidates are highly correlated | Current chunk plus the previous 3 or 7 still overlapping | Free if `n_action_steps = 1`; low diversity and older information. Diagnostic only |
| **E. Observation noise** (joint-state noise, image crop or colour jitter) | No | Yes, input-side | Uncontrolled | z = 0 on clean input plus N − 1 noisy inputs | N forward passes; diversity not tied to anything physical. Not recommended |

**Recommendation.** Two proposal sources, both anchored on candidate 0 = the deterministic z = 0 chunk:
1. **Structured perturbations (B) are the primary ACT condition.** They need no retraining, their size is controlled, and the only published head-to-head (simulation) found headroom there and none in native samples.
2. **Latent sampling (A) at τ = 1 is the native-sample condition, conditional on an offline gate:** if fewer than 80% of decision points on held-out logged observations reach the distinctness threshold (proposed default), ACT's native condition is dropped and reported as a finding (ACT's prior carries too little test-time diversity on this task), and the native-vs-structured comparison moves to π0.5.

**π0.5 (second candidate, later, once a compatible export exists).** ACT comes first in every work order (§5). π0.5 produces continuous actions with a flow-matching action expert, starting from Gaussian noise and running 10 denoising steps (verified, https://arxiv.org/html/2504.16054, read 2026-11-08). Native candidates are different noise seeds, as in VeriVLA. Its N = 1 baseline is one sample with a logged seed; candidate 0 is that sample. The same distinctness metric, nesting, perturbation menu and hard-violation rules apply. Inference memory ("about 10 GB") and GPU needs are review leads, unverified here; π0.5 on the Mac is expected to be infeasible for closed-loop timing (estimated), which makes a bench GPU or rented card a spending question.

## 3. The real-state scorer

### 3.1 What it carries over from M1, and what must change
M1's scorer (WO-005, WO-016) rolls a 3-member residual ensemble (`src/vishwaroop/models/compute_pilot.py`, `cube_study.py`) over a 13-value state, computes knock, drop and table margins (`src/vishwaroop/scoring/violations.py`), scales intervals by split-conformal calibration (`conformal_scale` in `m1_scoring.py`), and abstains above the 90th-percentile calibrated cube std (`score_branches.py`). The RSSM in `latent_predictor.py` is the alternative backbone. M1 has no hard-violation path; every violation was a scored margin. Changes for real state:
- **No grasp flag.** `object_grasped` is M1-only by owner decision (`docs/CONTEXT_AND_DECISIONS.md`); "held" must be inferred from gripper joint, tool-to-object distance and object height.
- **Pre-grasp knocks.** The review reports that the plain-A gate zeroes predicted displacement of an unheld object, which hides the most common violation (37 to 42 of 44 to 50). Treat as a lead to verify in code, then fix before training on real data.
- **SO-101 kinematics** replace `so100_kinematics`; the nominal model comes from measured servo lag on our arm.
- **Scored prefix** H = the executed `n_action_steps` (proposed: at most 30 steps), not ACT's full 100-step chunk; M1 validated horizons up to 10 steps at 20 Hz only.
- **Hard constraints** (below) are new. Some are deterministic checks on commanded targets and stream health; others are predictions from the scorer with their own threshold.

### 3.2 Interface and decision outcomes (proposed)
- **Inputs:** joint positions (6, rad) and their recent history; commanded joint targets; object pose from the top camera (AprilTag or colour, position in m plus yaw), declared `role: reference_only`, which means it never enters the VLA's inputs; target region from the printed placement template (constant per placement); the candidate chunk; stream timestamps; servo load or current readings if the bus reports them (to be checked on our arm).
- **Outputs per candidate:** progress (predicted reduction in tool-to-object plus object-to-target distance over the prefix, from `task_cost`); soft violation margins (knock, drop, table) with a violation probability; a hard-violation flag with the constraint it breaches; uncertainty (ensemble spread, conformally scaled).
- **Output per decision:** one of three outcomes, always with a reason code.

| Outcome | When | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **Select** | Scorer in range, uncertainty narrow, no hard violation predicted for candidate 0, and the best candidate that passes the hard checks beats candidate 0 by the frozen margin | Execute the selected chunk |
| **Ordinary abstention** | The scorer lacks confidence to pick: out-of-range state, wide uncertainty, no candidate clearly better than candidate 0, a soft latency overrun, or object pose briefly missing within the grace window. Nothing predicts or detects a hard violation for candidate 0 | Execute candidate 0, the policy's standard z = 0 chunk. The system reduces to N = 1 for that decision |
| **Hard violation → safe stop or handoff** | A hard constraint (table below) is predicted for candidate 0 or detected in the state stream, including the case where every candidate, candidate 0 included, is predicted to violate | Do **not** execute candidate 0. Enter a safe stop, then hand off to the operator |

**Hard constraints (each a *proposed default*, frozen with the protocol before any real scoring).**

| ID | Hard constraint | How it is judged | Proposed default |
|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | Joint-limit or workspace breach | Deterministic: commanded targets in the prefix against the official URDF joint limits less a margin; end-effector position (SO-101 FK) against a declared workspace box. Also detected from measured joint positions during execution | margin 0.05 rad inside each limit; workspace box measured on the bench |
| H2 | Table or fixture collision | Predicted: lower interval bound of tool and link clearance to the table plane or a declared fixture | clearance below 5 mm |
| H3 | Predicted knock or drop above a hard margin | Predicted: displacement of an unheld object, or release of a held object away from the target, well beyond the soft scoring thresholds | unheld displacement > 30 mm (3× the 10 mm soft knock threshold); release > 50 mm above the support surface outside the target region |
| H4 | Gripper force or current limit | Detected: servo load or current reading on the gripper or any joint above a limit, sustained for a short window | limit and window set from a bench measurement on our arm (not yet measured; depends on what the bus reports) |
| H5 | Loss of the state or pose stream needed to judge safety | Detected: joint state stale, or object pose missing beyond the grace window | joint state older than 3 control periods; object pose missing > 0.5 s (shorter gaps are ordinary abstention) |
| H6 | Latency overrun past a hard deadline | Detected: no checked decision (candidate 0 plus hard checks) ready by the hard deadline after the chunk boundary | soft budget = S7 latency target (overrun → ordinary abstention); hard deadline = 2× the soft budget (overrun → safe stop) |

A prediction counts as a hard violation when its calibrated probability reaches p_stop, *proposed default* 0.3, set on the calibration split only. p_stop sits below the selection threshold on purpose: missing a hard violation costs more than a false stop.

**Decision order (proposed default).**
1. Stream and deadline checks (H5, H6). Fail → safe stop.
2. Candidate 0 is generated first and checked against H1 to H4. Predicted or detected hard violation → safe stop. A safer-looking alternative is **not** selected in its place, because alternative ranking is not validated offline (§0.2; §6, O11).
3. Every other candidate that fails H1 to H3 is removed.
4. If the scorer is in range, uncertainty is narrow, and a remaining candidate beats candidate 0 by the frozen margin → select it. Otherwise → ordinary abstention, execute candidate 0.
5. During execution, the detected checks (H1 on measured joints, H4, H5) keep running every control step and can trigger a safe stop mid-chunk.

**Safe stop (proposed default; must be checked against the SO-101's servo behaviour).** Stop issuing new joint targets. Each servo holds its last commanded position under torque. Torque is not cut, because the arm would sag and drop a held object. The gripper keeps its last command, so a held object is not released. The episode pauses and the operator is alerted. Points to check on the real arm before this definition is trusted: servo lag means the arm may still travel to the last target after the stop decision, so that travel must be measured (the alternative is to command the measured present position once); how the STS3215 behaves under sustained hold and its overload protection; and whether backlash lets the arm settle under gravity. All of these need arm motion and are not authorized.

**Handoff (proposed default).** After a safe stop the operator decides: resume by teleop, reset, or end the episode. The episode is logged with outcome `safe_stop` and, if the operator acts, `handoff`, with the hard-constraint ID and the operator action. In offline replay both are decision labels only; nothing moves.

**Independence (`AGENTS.md §13.2`).** The trial's final success assessment never uses the scorer's pose stream; it uses the frozen evidence table (load cell or break-beam plus blinded video, `docs/EVALUATION_PROTOCOL.md §4`).

### 3.3 Training data (estimated needs; all from imported real episodes)
Sources, via the real-episode importer and WO-006: owner teleop (successes and safe failures or interventions), the arm project's ACT evaluation rollouts, later π0.5 rollouts. Every source is real and executed; nothing imagined is relabelled.
- *Proposed default size:* at least 150 episodes over at least 5 sessions and at least 2 policy families, with at least 30 violating chunk windows in the test split (the M1 scorer needed a 10-violation minimum; 30 gives a usable AUROC interval). **Estimated:** at 30 Hz and about 20 s per episode, about 600 steps and about 20 non-overlapping 30-step windows per episode, about 3,000 windows in all.
- *Proposed split:* **train** = teleop plus ACT run-family A from sessions 1 to 3; **calibration** = session 4, all sources, perturbation-like variety included; **test** = sessions 5 and later, plus a held-out policy family (π0.5, or an independently trained ACT run, never an adjacent checkpoint). `audit_splits.py` must pass.
- **Labels:** soft violations computed from the reference pose stream with the M1 thresholds converted to the real bench (knock 10 mm, drop rules) only after the pose noise floor is measured. Hard violations (H1 to H6) labelled from the same streams plus servo logs and timestamps. A double-labelled sample of about 20 episodes against blinded video checks the automatic labels (`docs/EVALUATION_PROTOCOL.md §4`). Hard violations may be rare in logged data; no new ones are provoked to fill the count, because that would need arm motion.

### 3.4 Offline evaluation, frozen before any scoring (all proposed defaults; executed chunks only)

| Gate | Metric | Proposed default | Source of the default |
|---|---|---|---|
| S0 pose floor | Static jitter of the top-camera object pose | std ≤ 2 mm and ≤ 1/5 of the knock threshold | new; a 10 mm knock is meaningless under centimetre noise |
| S1 prediction | Multi-step RMSE vs constant, linear and nominal baselines, horizons 1..H, held-out sessions | ≥ 20% below the best baseline at every horizon | mirrors ET-M1-1 |
| S2 violation | AUROC for a soft violation in the executed chunk, on chunks kept | ≥ 0.75, bootstrap over sessions | mirrors ET-M1-5 |
| S3 ranking on executed outcomes | Spearman(predicted progress, realized progress) over executed chunks; pairwise ordering accuracy for executed chunks from matched placements whose realized progress differs by more than the pose floor | ρ ≥ 0.5; ordering ≥ 75% | new; executed chunks only (§0.2) |
| S4 calibration | 90% interval coverage per stream and horizon; ECE of violation probability | coverage within ±10 points; ECE ≤ 0.10 | mirrors ET-M1-3 |
| S5 ordinary abstention | Coverage on the test split; abstention on deliberately out-of-range inputs (held-out family, pose dropout within the grace window, unseen object position) | abstain ≤ 20% in range; abstention rate higher out of range than in range, with an interval | M1 cap; ET-M2a-4 shape |
| S6 false optimism | Fraction of actually violating executed chunks that the scorer kept and rated safe at the frozen threshold; plus, episode level, failed episodes rated as progressing at every decision | ≤ 10% | ET-M2a-4 proposed default |
| S7 latency | Wall time for candidate generation + scoring + selection, per candidate and per N ∈ {1, 4, 8}, p50/p95/max over ≥ 1,000 decisions, on the Mac (CPU and MPS) and on the bench computer | p95 at N = 8 ≤ min(100 ms, ¼ of the executed-prefix duration) on the bench computer | new; frozen once the export states the control rate |
| S8 hard-violation recall | Fraction of executed real chunks with a labelled hard violation (H1 to H6) that the detector flags at or before the step it occurs; Wilson interval | ≥ 0.90, and the interval's lower bound ≥ 0.75; at least 20 hard-violation chunks in the test split, otherwise reported as underpowered, not passed | new |
| S9 false safe-stop rate | Fraction of executed in-range chunks with no hard violation that would have triggered a safe stop; and the fraction of successful episodes that would have been stopped at least once | ≤ 2% of chunks; ≤ 10% of successful episodes; out-of-range rates reported separately | new |
| S10 stop-decision latency | Time from the last state sample to the safe-stop decision, p50/p95/max over ≥ 1,000 decisions, on the bench computer | deterministic checks (H1, H4, H5, H6) p95 ≤ one control period (33 ms at an estimated 30 Hz); predicted checks (H2, H3) within the S7 budget; zero hard-deadline misses in replay | new |

S8 is measurable offline because, in ACT rollouts, the executed chunk *is* candidate 0. Recall for unexecuted alternatives cannot be measured from logs. The deterministic checks (H1, H5, H6) are also unit-tested on fabricated streams (mocked/fixture); fixture results never count toward S8 to S10.

**What offline evidence cannot show about the safe stop.** Replay can show that the stop *decision* is correct and fast enough. It cannot show what the arm does next: how far it travels after the decision, whether it holds position under load, whether a held object stays held, how the servos' overload protection reacts, or how quickly an operator takes over. That needs a separately approved physical check (§4). Until then, the safe stop is a design, not a verified behaviour.

**Latency anchors already in this repo (measured, M1, Mac M3 Pro, simulated state):** a CEM decision with 3 members, 256 samples, horizon 20, 5 iterations took 158 ms on MPS and 169 ms on CPU, and 87 / 67 ms at 128 samples and 3 iterations (`reports/WO-012-m1-compute-split.md`); the M1 planners took 103 ms (learned) and 90 ms (nominal) median per control step under contention, against a 50 ms period (`reports/WO-005-m1-planner-and-chunk-scorer.md`); scoring 764 chunks offline took about 5 s wall time including loading (`reports/WO-016-m1-et5-retest.md`), which is not a per-decision latency. Per-candidate scorer latency for control has **not been measured**. **Estimated:** one batched rollout of 8 candidates should cost about one CEM iteration's dispatch overhead, roughly 30 ms on this Mac (158 ms / 5 iterations), before the review's suggested fixes; ACT's batched forward for N candidates is not run. Report both the median and the induced pause (Q4).

A failed gate is reported as a negative result; thresholds are not moved after seeing scores.

## 4. What a physical-trial proposal would contain later (not planned for execution)

A separate document, written only after §3.4 passes, sent to the owner, frozen before any episode. Any physical trial, and any safe-stop behaviour on the real arm, comes back to the owner for separate approval. A trial run before M2b passes, if the owner approves one, would be a pilot and would not count toward ET-M5-1 or ET-M5-2; M5's own trials wait for M2b and a frozen M5 protocol (§0.1).
- **Safe-stop check first (proposed, not authorized):** before any policy trial, a short scripted test with no policy and low speed that triggers each detectable stop (H1, H4, H5, H6) and measures travel after the decision, hold under load and gripper retention. It needs its own hardware-motion approval.
- **Arms (proposed):** ACT N = 1 (standard output, with the same hard-violation monitor); ACT N = 4 and N = 8 with the recommended source; at N = 8, native vs structured if both pass the distinctness gate; optionally a latency-matched N = 1 (the same pause, no selection) to isolate Q4. π0.5 arms only once its export and inference hardware exist.
- **Design:** matched placements from the printed template with image-overlay checks; randomized arm order within each placement block; blocks spread across sessions; at least 30 episodes per arm, the final count from a frozen power plan on expected discordant pairs (`docs/EVALUATION_PROTOCOL.md §5`). **Estimated:** 4 arms × 30 = 120 episodes, about 4 to 6 hours of arm time at 2 to 3 minutes per episode with reset.
- **Outcomes:** independent success assessment per the frozen evidence table and blinded video; Wilson intervals; McNemar with paired effect intervals; session-clustered resampling. Each episode ends as exactly one of: success, failure, safe stop (with or without handoff), or invalid trial. *Proposed default:* a safe-stopped episode is not a success in the primary metric, and it is also reported on its own line.
- **Always reported, each on its own line per arm:** ordinary abstention rate per arm and per decision, with reason codes; safe stops per arm, per episode and per hard constraint (H1 to H6), with the operator's handoff action; failures without a safe stop; false optimism (decisions where the selected chunk was predicted safe and the episode failed at that step); hard violations that occurred with no safe stop (missed detections); per-decision latency and pause; compute per episode; interventions and invalid trials with reasons. Abstentions, safe stops, handoffs and failures are never pooled. Selection regret needs every candidate executed from the same state, which is a separate, smaller sub-study if wanted.
- **Matched-budget comparator (Q1):** the arm project fine-tunes the same policy on B extra real episodes, with B equal to the real episodes the verifier route consumed for scorer training and calibration.
- **Separate owner approvals it needs (`AGENTS.md §2.3`):** hardware motion (every session, the safe-stop check included); deployment of a changed decision path that acts on the arm (the selector and its safe-stop logic), including where the runtime lives, since editing the arm project is itself a §2.3 item; the frozen protocol and exit tests; any spending (π0.5 inference GPU, rented or bought); a sealed-suite opening if one is used.

## 6. Risks and open questions for the owner

**Risks**
- **R1 ACT's latent may be collapsed** (`kl_weight = 10`), making native ACT candidates near-identical. Mitigated by the offline gate in §2; a negative is a result.
- **R2 Pose noise and occlusion.** A single top camera loses the object under the gripper, exactly when knocks and drops happen (per the review, ET-M3-1 failed four times because the arm occludes a fixed camera). S0 must pass; abstaining on missing pose must count against coverage. Long gaps also trigger H5, so occlusion drives the false safe-stop rate (S9).
- **R3 Offline accuracy does not license counterfactual ranking** (§0.2). The trial remains the only real test of selection.
- **R4 Distribution shift to perturbed candidates.** Executed logs contain few perturbed chunks; calibration may be over-optimistic on them (WO-005's abstention failure). Teleop safe failures and multiple policy families are the partial mitigation.
- **R5 Latency changes behaviour.** DREAM-Chunk's remote π0.5 runs (RTX A6000 over WebSocket, about 0.7 to 1.3 s per decision) lost their gains to delay; a pause at chunk boundaries on a backlash-limited arm may itself change outcomes. Hence the latency-matched control.
- **R6 Data volume.** 150 episodes over 5 sessions is weeks of bench time (estimated) and depends on the arm project exporting.
- **R7 Small effects.** With 30 episodes per arm, only large paired differences are detectable; a null result must be reported as such.
- **R8 Few hard violations in logged data.** S8 needs at least 20 labelled hard-violation chunks; logs from a working policy may hold fewer. Then S8 is reported as underpowered, and the trial proposal cannot claim the detector is validated.
- **R9 Safe-stop physical behaviour is unknown.** Servo lag, hold under load, overload protection and backlash sag are not measured. Offline gates cannot close this (§3.4).
- **R10 Over-stopping.** A conservative p_stop and a short pose grace window could stop many good episodes, which would read as a success loss. S9 bounds this offline.

**Open questions**
- **O1 (answered 2026-11-08).** Decoupling M5-early from M2b is not approved. M5 keeps its M2b dependency; M5-early is offline preparation that does not count toward any M5 exit test; any physical trial comes back for separate approval (§0.1).
- **O2** The fine-tuning comparator: SFT on B extra real episodes in the arm project, or wait for an RL fine-tune? Who counts B, and in what unit? ET-M5-1 names the GRPO-tuned policy as a comparator; the matched-budget SFT arm would be an addition.
- **O3** Where does the selection runtime live when a trial is approved: inside the arm project (a §2.3 modification) or a vishwaroop runner that commands the arm (a new hardware-motion boundary)? The safe-stop logic lives wherever the selector lives.
- **O4** If the exported ACT uses temporal ensembling, should N = 1 keep it (faithful baseline) or match the best-of-N arms' chunk-boundary execution (cleaner comparison)? Recommendation: keep it, and add the matched variant only if arm time allows.
- **O5** Which bench computer runs the latency harness, and may an agent run software on it with no actuator connected?
- **O6** π0.5 inference hardware: rented card within a future approved envelope, or wait for the workstation decision?
- **O7** Confirm the distinctness threshold source: our measured backlash rather than the literature lead.
- **O8** Confirm the hard-constraint list H1 to H6 and their proposed margins, or add to it.
- **O9** Safe-stop definition: hold the last commanded target (proposed), or command the measured present position once? Either needs the physical check in §4 before use.
